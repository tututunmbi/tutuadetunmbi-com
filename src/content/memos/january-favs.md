# A Book I Read:

##### ***The Go-Giver: A Little Story About a Powerful Business Idea***

##### ***By Bob Burg & John David Mann***

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/ea93e8d9-917b-45c1-acfd-23bf429e0825_255x400.heic)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xLgw!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fea93e8d9-917b-45c1-acfd-23bf429e0825_255x400.heic)

**The Go-Giver** is an absolutely stellar book—a must-read for everyone! I finished it feeling so inspired that I immediately started making notes on how to imbibe the *go-giver* life.

What makes this book so powerful is its refreshing approach to success—focusing on *giving* and *providing value* rather than just striving to get ahead. It follows Joe, an ambitious salesman who finds himself in dire straits after a deal he was counting on falls through.

Through what feels like divine intervention, Joe meets Bob, who teaches him an unconventional path to success: *give away what you are best at instead of focusing solely on what you want from others.* Bob introduces him to a series of mentors, each demonstrating one of the **Five Laws of Stratospheric Success**:

### **The Five Laws of Stratospheric Success:**

🔹 **The Law of Value:** Your true worth is determined by how much more you give in value than you take in payment.  
🔹 **The Law of Compensation:** Your income is determined by how many people you serve and how well you serve them.  
🔹 **The Law of Influence:** Your influence grows when you abundantly place other people’s interests first.  
🔹 **The Law of Authenticity:** The most valuable gift you have to offer is yourself.  
🔹 **The Law of Receptivity:** The key to effective giving is staying open to receiving.

If I had to describe *The Go-Giver* in one line? *It’s a book that teaches you how to be a better person.*

I know that may sound strong, but it’s true.

This book left a mark on me, and I know it will on you too. If you read *The Go-Giver*, let’s talk about it! I’d love to hear your thoughts.

# A Netflix Documentary That Shocked Me:

#### *Kings of Tupelo*: A Southern Crime Saga

[![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/873be829-6fd1-49ac-a14a-af7b69ebda43_1600x900.heic)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pGeW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F873be829-6fd1-49ac-a14a-af7b69ebda43_1600x900.heic)

This is hands down the most fascinating doc I’ve seen in a while. It has that same chaotic energy as *Tiger King* (2020)—full of bizarre, over-the-top characters and a story that feels almost too wild to be real. I was hooked from the first episode and binged it in a day. At first, I was thinking, *what the hell am I watching?* But by the end, I was *fully* invested.

Basically, this documentary is about Kevin—an Elvis impersonator whose work dries up, so he takes a janitor job. One day, he stumbles upon a *severed head*, which convinces him that human body parts are being trafficked. Before long, he gets mixed up in a plot to send ricin to the President. I mean... **what?!** You just have to watch this! Everyone in the documentary is stark raving mad, hilarious and you just can’t help but marvel at their weirdness.

Absolutely bonkers, but ridiculously entertaining. Have you watched it?

# What did January Teach Me?

1. **Don’t be scared of being triggered.** Eventually, you realize the best way to deal with everything is to *face* everything. Rewire your brain to welcome triggers with excitement. They can be reflections of where you need to grow, but they can also be signals of your boundaries—what you will and won’t allow. Acknowledging and learning from your triggers shouldn’t scare you; it’s the only way to see what needs healing. It gives you a chance to learn who you are at the core. And once you take control of your triggers, other people lose control over you. Now *you* have the buttons, so no one can push them. Self-analysis. Self-honesty. It’s all growth. Perspective is everything.
2. **Answered prayers come in the form of preparation.** Tests, challenges, and obstacles show up to see if you can steward the blessing you prayed for. Be discerning, and be thankful when they come. It will hurt, but you need to be pruned to blossom.
3. **Allow people to be who they are.** You cannot change people. Not only is it selfish, but it’s also an effort in futility. Know who you are, go where aligns, and let people be who they are. Your only choice is whether you want to be there or not.
4. There’s a difference between being alone and being lonely.
5. **Hard work alone doesn’t equate to success.** Smart choices about what you prioritise account for 99% of the difference. It’s what you know and who you know. And that gap? It’s often bridged by people who genuinely want to help others succeed. So, long story short—**NETWORK.**
6. **Your deepest wisdom is your unfair advantage.** The mindset you have. The belief system life forced you to develop. The person you became because of the good and bad you’ve experienced. Your unique view of the world. That’s your unfair advantage.
7. **Feeling sorry for yourself is a waste of time.** No one is exempt from pain, loss, disappointment, or sorrow. Being sad occasionally is normal and healthy. But dwelling on your misfortunes? That’s self-destruction. There is *always* light at the end of the tunnel. Thinking others have it better is unhealthy and time-consuming. It’s also not easy—it invites loneliness, anger, and resentment. Once you start feeling like a victim, you start behaving like one.
8. **Being truly yourself and knowing what you want is rare.** Having a strong conviction is something, not many people understand or relate to.
9. **Authors don’t get ideas without inspiration.** Your dreams, your wants, your standards, your desires—they are not far-fetched for the mere fact that you *dream* them!
10. **We don’t get to choose who we are—we only get to discover who we are.**
