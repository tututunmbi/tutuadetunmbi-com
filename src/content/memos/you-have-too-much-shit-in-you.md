The **constipation** of your own potential will leave you unable to give a **shit**, *and yes, I like that play on words*, so we are going to stay in this bathroom for a while.

…

So these days, I’ve become a little obsessed with a show called *[Intervention](https://youtu.be/XY8uXjBnK98?si=rH6u_98ICfUQ6K1-)*.

You meet people struggling with substance abuse, hear from them and their families, and watch as the people who love them try to get them to accept help. Sometimes they go into treatment, sometimes they refuse, and sometimes you find yourself hoping for someone you have known for forty minutes with an intensity that feels slightly unreasonable until you remember that you’re watching an actual person try to stay alive…

After a while, I started noticing how often people spoke about “*potential”*. How talented someone had been, how bright, how much they had ahead of them. Somewhere in the telling of their story, the language would shift towards everything they could have become, *****and I kept thinking about what it must feel like to sit in a room while people describe the life they thought you would be living.*****

“*Could have been*” is a difficult thing to hear about yourself, especially when you’re still here.

There was one young woman in particular who stayed with me. As far as I remember, she spoke about wanting to be a star, to sing, to act, to write a book and travel the world. Her parents said she was so smart and could have been *“anything she wanted to be”*. I don’t know her beyond what the programme let me see, and I wouldn’t pretend that a thought I had on my sofa could explain her addiction. But I recognized something in the weight of all those possible lives, because I know what it feels like to have so many ideas about who you could become that you can barely find room for the person who has to get through today.

And I suspect quite a few of us know that feeling…

> “*****Potential*****” is supposed to be the healthy part of the diet. It’s the greens, the legumes, the thing everybody keeps telling you is so good for you. You have so much of it. People can see it in you. You can even see it in yourself. So you keep taking it in, every idea, every skill, every possibility, every compliment about what you could do if you really put your mind to it, **without noticing that very little is coming out.**
> 
> **Eventually, you’re backed up with entire lives you haven’t lived. Discomforted by the constipation that has been caused from eating too much “potential”.**

One version of you wants to be CEO, another wants to audition for a show. Another is quite certain you should be selling this new skill as a side hustle. Somebody wants to write a book, although nobody on this committee can tell you what the book is about. Then there’s the one who wants you to start posting content, but apparently we can’t just post something today. **We have to account for all the content we should have been posting since we first had the idea in 2010.**

Every version of you has an urgent request, and they have all decided that the person responsible for fulfilling it is the same person who still needs to answer their emails, reply messages, look good, be present for friends and family, and work out how they will live a long and healthy life **㋛**.

**I think there comes a point when all that possibility begins to feel like an accusation.** You can’t enjoy being good at something without wondering why you haven’t done more with it. You have an interesting thought, and before you’ve even enjoyed having it, you’re calculating how far behind you are in turning your interesting thoughts into a career. Even a compliment can become uncomfortable, because *“you have so much potential”* starts sounding suspiciously like *“we are still waiting.”*

And this is where the constipation begins to interfere with your ability to give a shit.

From the outside, it might look as though you’ve become lazy, or lost interest, or made your peace with leaving certain things alone. Sometimes you might even tell yourself that you don’t care anymore, because it’s easier than explaining how much you care and how little you seem able to do with it.

But I wonder how often that indifference is exhaustion. How often a person has cared about too many things for too long, with nowhere for any of that care to go, until turning down the volume becomes the only way to get through the day.

**You stop opening the document because opening it means encountering the book you haven’t written. You avoid the camera because you can already see three years of videos you never made. The thing in front of you has become so crowded with everything that came before it that attempting it feels like agreeing to confront your entire life.**

No wonder you’re tired! You sat down to write a paragraph and accidentally summoned a tribunal.

Now let me ask you a serious question. **Can you shit the shit you were supposed to shit seven days ago?** 😂 Are you going to sit there organizing separate bowel movements for Monday, Tuesday, and the person you were supposed to become in 2019? NO YOU CAN’T…You sit on the toilet and deal with what is there. Whatever you’ve been carrying, you can only let it out now.

> And yet, when it comes to our lives, we keep trying to schedule a retrospective evacuation. We want today’s effort to account for everything we didn’t do yesterday, last year, and during that period when we were telling everybody we were *“working on something.”* You finally sit down to write, but instead of dealing with the page in front of you, you’re trying to produce the entire career you believe you should already have.

So you want to write a book? Write a couple of pages every day. You want to try? Try today. Take one thing, *****one shit*****, and give your whole shit to that, because **you can’t shit all the shits you haven’t shat before. You can only shit the shit that you can shit today.**

I know how ridiculous that sounds, but stay with me, because we seem perfectly willing to accept that some things require regular attention until those things involve our ambitions. Then suddenly, we want one enormous annual event. We’re going to disappear, get everything together, and return with something so spectacular that it will explain the silence, justify the delay, and establish us as the person we’ve spent all this time promising to become.

*Meanwhile, there is something small we could finish before dinner.*

We all know the journey of a thousand miles starts with a single step, and we nod along, and then we turn around and expect that one step to cover the thousand miles. When it obviously can’t, we decide there’s no point in taking it. But you only get there by taking one step, and another, and another, and none of them will ever feel like enough while you’re measuring each one against the whole distance.

Of course a couple of pages will look unimpressive next to the three books you think you should have published by now. But those pages are available to you. You can write them, read them, discover what you’re trying to say, and come back tomorrow with a little more understanding. The three imaginary books have had years to become extraordinary without ever having to survive contact with a reader.

And I’m including myself here, because I know what it is to make the available attempt feel so small that I almost resent having to make it. I want to get straight to the part where the work reflects everything I know I have in me. Unfortunately, having it in me is becoming the problem. At some point, something has to come out.

You have to give a shit about your life, and you have to give a shit about your potential. The trick, as far as I can tell, is that you can only give them out one at a time. You give a shit about this one thing, you let it go, and you move to the next one and the next one.

Some of what comes out will disappoint you. You might discover that an idea needs more work, or that you don’t enjoy something nearly as much as you enjoyed imagining yourself doing it. You might also make something that finds its people, starts a conversation, or gives you a reason to keep going. *Either way, you have something to respond to beyond the committee in your head and its increasingly unreasonable demands.*

What stays with me about that girl is that all of it seemed to be still inside her, every star and every book and every country, with no room left for the day she was actually in. I don’t know what happened beyond what the programme showed me, and I hope she got the help she needed. But that feeling of carrying so many possible selves that you can barely move as the person you are is something I brought back into my own life.

You can’t go back and give the person you were seven days ago a different day. You can sit down, give a shit about one thing, and come back tomorrow. And I have started to think that being regular is the only way any of us gets to keep giving a shit at all.
